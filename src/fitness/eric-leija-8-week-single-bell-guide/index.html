Bells

The PDF never says what weight to use, so each move is marked light, medium or heavy for how the bell should feel to you rather than a number. A chip pair like Light→Medium means start with the lighter bell and move up when it feels solid.

Heavy

Heavy: Slow two-hand lifts

Two-hand deadlifts (Workout 2) and the deep squat hold (Workout 10). Not much else, at least at first.

Medium

Medium: Your main bell

Rows, cleans, alternating swings, racked split squats, single-leg deadlifts and explosive deadlifts. Start the explosive deadlifts here, not with the heavy bell, since you land jumps holding it.

Light

Light: Anything overhead

Presses, arm bars, windmills, half get-ups, overhead sit-ups, tripod presses, snatches and halos. Also the curl and triceps finisher in Workout 5.

BW

Bodyweight

Everything else. "On bell" means the bell lies on its side as a push-up handle; "target" means it's only something to reach for.

Picking your bells

Choose each so the last few reps of a set are hard but your form holds, and go lighter when unsure. Overhead work takes the least weight, rows and cleans more, and two-hand deadlifts the most. Beginner guides suggest a first bell of about 8–12 kg for women and 12–16 kg for men: make that your medium, with a lighter one for overhead work and a heavier one for deadlifts. The colors follow the competition kettlebell code for a common 12, 16 and 24 kg set: blue, yellow and green.

Set up before each circuit

There's only about 10 seconds between moves, so put both bells you need within reach before the circuit starts.

No room to jump?

  • Explosive deadlift or hinge jump → two-hand swing
  • Plyo lunges → reverse lunges
  • Squat or chamber jumps → fast bodyweight squats
  • Lateral jumps → lateral lunges
  • 10-yard sprints → fast high knees in place

Timer keys

While the timer runs and no button or link has focus, Space pauses or resumes, ← goes back a step and → skips to the next one.

Where the demos come from

Most demos are Eric Leija's own, from his YouTube channel and the short clips on ericleija.com. Gaps are filled from Onnit Academy, Kettlebell Athletes, StrongFirst, NASM and a few other coaches; each ▶ names its source. Times like "2:01" jump to the moment the move starts, taken from the video's own description. A ≈ marks a close match rather than the exact move.

Standing lateral reach (a rest move in Workout 5) is the only move without a demo.
